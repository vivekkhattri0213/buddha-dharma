How to Use This Repo

Time: 2 minutes

Every lesson has the same shape

So you never have to figure out the layout:

  1. Two-minute version: the whole lesson in a few lines. If that's all you have, stop here.
  2. Step by step: numbered, short, one idea each.
  3. Try it now: one tiny practice, under 5 minutes.
  4. Check yourself: a few questions with answers hidden under a click.
  5. Common mix-ups: what people usually get wrong.
  6. Go deeper: optional links. Never required.

Rules for yourself

  • One lesson per sitting.
  • Zero guilt for skipping sections. The two-minute version counts.
  • Do the "Try it now" even badly. Reading alone doesn't stick.
  • If a word is unfamiliar, check the glossary and keep going.
  • Don't try to believe anything yet. Treat each idea as a hypothesis to test.

A note on the Pali words

Pali terms (like dukkha) appear in italics once, with a plain English version. After that we mostly use English. Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu often translates dukkha as stress or suffering; we use both.

Why the Thai Forest tradition?

It stresses direct practice: meditation and watching your own mind, backed by the earliest texts (the Pali Canon). That fits a "try it and see" approach.

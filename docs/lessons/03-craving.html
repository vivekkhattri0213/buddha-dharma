Lesson 3: Craving, the 3 Kinds

Time: 7 minutes · Source: SN 56.11 (craving as the second noble truth)

Two-minute version

Craving (taṇhā, literally "thirst") is the cause of stress. The Buddha named three kinds:

# Pali Plain English Looks like
1 kāma-taṇhā Craving for sensual pleasure "I want that taste, scroll, touch, comfort"
2 bhava-taṇhā Craving for becoming "I want to be someone: successful, loved, spiritual"
3 vibhava-taṇhā Craving for non-becoming "I want to not be: disappear, cut it off, be rid of this"

All three are grasping for a particular state, toward or away.

Step by step

Step 1: Craving isn't all desire

The Buddha taught the desire to practice and the desire to be skillful. Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu often notes that the path itself starts with wanting to end suffering (see his study guide Desires). Craving is the grasping, stressed-out thirst, not every wish.

Step 2: Kind 1, Sensuality

Wanting pleasant sights, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and the fantasies and plans that chase them. Includes doom-scrolling and snacking when you're not hungry.

Step 3: Kind 2, Becoming

Wanting a role or identity: to be good at your job, admired, enlightened. It's subtle because it can look like ambition or even "being a good meditator."

Step 4: Kind 3, Non-becoming

Wanting to annihilate, escape, or numb: "I wish I didn't exist," or pushing away a feeling. It feels like the opposite of craving, but it's still thirst, for an ending.

Step 5: See the pattern

  1. Contact with something
  2. Feeling (pleasant / painful / neutral)
  3. Craving shows up
  4. Clinging follows (next lesson)

The Buddha's method: catch craving at step 3.

Try it now (3 minutes)

Write a quick list (phone notes is fine):

  • One thing I wanted today (sensuality)
  • One way I wanted to be seen/become (becoming)
  • One thing I wanted to escape or switch off (non-becoming)

No judgment. Labeling is the exercise.

Check yourself

Is "I want to escape this feeling" craving?Yes, non-becoming craving.
Does Buddhism say to stop all wanting?No. The craving that causes stress is dropped; skillful intentions are developed.
Which kind hides inside "self-improvement"?Often becoming craving.

Common mix-ups

  • "Non-becoming craving is good, since it's less attachment." It's another form of thirst.
  • "Craving is only for pleasure." Two of the three aren't about pleasure at all.

# Lesson 4: Clinging, the 4 Kinds

**Time: 7 minutes** · Source: [DN 15](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/DN/DN15.html) and [MN 11](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN11.html) (*upādāna*, "clinging")

## Two-minute version

Clinging (*upādāna*) is the **grabbing** that follows craving. It's also the word for **fuel**: a fire keeps burning as long as it keeps taking on fuel. The Buddha listed **four** kinds:

| # | Clinging to... | Plain English | Looks like |
|---|----------------|---------------|------------|
| 1 | **Sensuality** (*kāma*) | Pleasures and the plans for them | "I can't be happy without X" |
| 2 | **Views** (*diṭṭhi*) | Opinions held as "the truth" | "I'm right, and my take defines me" |
| 3 | **Habits & practices** (*sīlabbata*) | Rules and rituals treated as magic | "If I do this exactly, I'll be saved" |
| 4 | **Doctrine of self** (*attavāda*) | "This is what I am" | "I'm the kind of person who..." |

**Craving is the thirst; clinging is the grip.**

## Step by step

### Step 1: Connect it to Lesson 3
Craving shows up → you **grab** → that grab is clinging → stress follows. It's a chain, not a separate topic.

### Step 2: Clinging to sensuality
Holding on to the pleasures, and to the *hope* of them. The suffering comes when they end or don't arrive.

### Step 3: Clinging to views
Even "good" views. You can cling to a Buddhist view as a badge. Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu often points out that the Buddha taught views **as tools** to be used and then dropped (like a raft, MN 22), not as positions to defend.

### Step 4: Clinging to habits and practices
Treating a precept, ritual, or technique as **automatically** liberating, regardless of understanding. Practice matters; clinging to *the form itself* is the trap.

### Step 5: Clinging to a doctrine of self
The big one. It's the belief **"I am this"** (this body, this feeling, this role) and acting from it. Lesson 2's "not-self" reflection targets this.

### Step 6: The link to the first truth
In Lesson 1, Truth 1 summed up stress as **the five aggregates of clinging**. Those five (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) become *stressful* when they're **grabbed as "me/mine."** That's why clinging is the heart of the problem.

## Check yourself

<details><summary>What's the difference between craving and clinging?</summary>Craving is thirst or wanting; clinging is grasping and taking on as "mine," like fuel for a fire.</details>
<details><summary>Can you cling to a Buddhist teaching?</summary>Yes: that's clinging to views or to habits and practices.</details>
<details><summary>Which clinging is the core of suffering?</summary>The clinging to a doctrine of self, since it underlies the other identifications.</details>

## Common mix-ups

- **"Clinging = caring."** You can care without gripping.
- **"Views are bad."** Right view is part of the path; the problem is *clinging* to it.

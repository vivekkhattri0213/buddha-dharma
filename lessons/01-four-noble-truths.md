# Lesson 1: The Four Noble Truths

**Time: 10 minutes** · Main source: the Buddha's first sermon, [SN 56.11](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN56_11.html)

## Two-minute version

- The Buddha's first teaching wasn't "life is suffering, deal with it." It was a **method** for ending stress.
- There are four categories: **stress, its cause, its end, the path to its end.**
- Each one comes with a **job**:

| Truth | Plain meaning | Your job |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| 1. Dukkha | The stress of clinging | **Understand** it fully |
| 2. Origin | Craving | **Let go of** it |
| 3. Cessation | Craving fades, stress ends | **Realize** it |
| 4. Path | The eightfold path | **Develop** it |

Think of it as a doctor's approach: **diagnosis, cause, cure is possible, treatment plan.**

## Step by step

### Step 1: Notice the word "truth" is slightly misleading
"Noble truths" sounds like four statements to believe. Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu stresses that in the early texts they work more like **four categories for sorting your experience**, each with a task attached. You're not asked to agree. You're asked to do the job.

### Step 2: Truth 1, Stress (*dukkha*)
- The Buddha lists familiar things: birth, aging, illness, death, being with what you dislike, being apart from what you like, not getting what you want.
- Then he sums it up: **"in short, the five clustered objects of clinging are stress."**
- Translation: the heavy part is not the events themselves, but **how we grab onto body, feelings, perceptions, mental fabrications, and awareness as "mine" or "me."**
- **Job: comprehend it.** Look closely at where the weight is. Don't fix, don't flee. Just understand.

### Step 3: Truth 2, The cause (*samudaya*)
- The cause is **craving** (*taṇhā*): for sensual pleasure, for becoming something, for not-becoming.
- Not "desire" in general. The Buddha doesn't tell you to stop wanting to get out of bed, eat, or meditate. See [Lesson 3](03-craving.md).
- **Job: abandon it.** Let it go, not suppress it.

### Step 4: Truth 3, The ending (*nirodha*)
- Stress can end. It ends when craving is **fully let go of, relinquished, released.**
- This is the hopeful one. It says the problem is *solvable*.
- **Job: realize it.** It's something to experience, not to figure out by thinking.

### Step 5: Truth 4, The path (*magga*)
- The **noble eightfold path**: right view, resolve, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration.
- Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu groups these as a **skill** you build, like learning an instrument, especially the meditative factors (effort, mindfulness, concentration).
- **Job: develop it.** Practice.

### Step 6: Put the jobs in order in your head
Understand stress → drop craving → see the ending → keep building the path. In practice you loop through them many times.

## Try it now (3 minutes)

1. Think of something mildly annoying from today.
2. Ask: **"What exactly am I holding onto here?"** (a wish, a self-image, a plan)
3. Ask: **"Is the pain in the event itself, or in my grip on it?"**
4. That's Truth 1, *just looking*. You don't have to do anything else.

## Common mix-ups

- **"Buddhism is pessimistic."** The second half (end of stress) is the main point.
- **"Stop all desire."** Skillful desire (for wisdom, for goodness) fuels the path. Craving gets dropped *at the end*. See [Lesson 3](03-craving.md).
- **"Truths to believe."** They're tasks to perform.

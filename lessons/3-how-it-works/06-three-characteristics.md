# Lesson 6: The Three Characteristics

**Time: 10 minutes** · Sources: [AN 3.136](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN3_136.html) and [SN 22.59](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN22_59.html) (*Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta*)

## Two-minute version

Three labels for the things you tend to cling to (body, feelings, thoughts, etc.):

1. **Inconstant** (*anicca*): it changes.
2. **Stressful** (*dukkha*): clinging to something that changes hurts.
3. **Not-self** (*anattā*): not worth treating as "me, mine, what I am."

Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's key point: these are used as **tools for letting go**, applied to what you're clinging to, rather than as claims about the nature of reality. Use them when you notice grasping.

## Step by step

### Step 1: Inconstant
- Everything you can experience (a mood, a thought, a sensation, your body) is **put together from causes and keeps changing**.
- Practice question: *"Has this stayed the same for even a minute?"*

### Step 2: Stressful
- Then the Buddha asks: **"What is inconstant, is it easy or stressful?"** Stressful.
- Why: if you rely on something that won't stay, relying on it hurts.
- Practice question: *"If I'm hoping this stays, what happens when it goes?"*

### Step 3: Not-self
- Next: **"What is inconstant, stressful, and subject to change: is it fitting to regard it as 'This is mine, this is my self, this is what I am'?"** No.
- In the *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta* this is followed by a **test of control**: "If the body were your self, it wouldn't lead to affliction, and you could say 'let my body be like this.'" You can't. So it isn't something to claim as *you*.
- Practice question: *"Is it worth identifying with this?"*

### Step 4: Notice the goal
After this reflection, the text says the listener becomes **dispassionate**, and, **through dispassion, released**. The point is **freedom from clinging**.

### Step 5: Notice what "not-self" is and isn't
- It's a **strategy**: drop the "I am this" identification and see what remains.
- Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu points out that the Buddha **declined to answer** the question "is there a self or not?" (see the Vacchagotta exchange in [SN 44.10](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN44_10.html)), because either answer leads to more clinging to a view.
- So: don't adopt "there is no self" as a new belief to cling to.

## Try it now (4 minutes)

1. Sit and notice **one** feeling in the body (tight shoulders, warm hands).
2. Watch it for one minute. **Does it change?** (inconstant)
3. Notice whether you're *wanting* it to be different or to stay. **Is that comfortable?** (stressful)
4. Ask: **"Do I need to *be* this?"** Let the answer be small and honest. (not-self)

## Common mix-ups

- **"Nothing exists."** No. It's about what's worth clinging to.
- **"I must feel detached."** The aim isn't numbness; it's not being pulled around by grasping.
- **"Not-self means I'm worthless."** It's about dropping identification, not self-criticism.
